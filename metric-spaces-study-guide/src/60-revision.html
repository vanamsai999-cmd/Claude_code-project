Trap questions: true or false?

Examiners like these. Decide first, then open each one to check. Every "false" comes with a counterexample, which is what an answer needs.

1. Every subset of a metric space is either open or closed.
False. $[0,1[$ and $\Q$ in $\R$ are neither. Some sets are both: $\emptyset$, $E$, and every subset of a discrete space.
2. An arbitrary intersection of open sets is open.
False. $\bigcap_n]-1/n,1/n[=\{0\}$. Only finite intersections of open sets are open; arbitrary unions are open.
3. An arbitrary union of closed sets is closed.
False. $\bigcup_n[1/n,1]=\,]0,1]$. Only finite unions of closed sets are closed; arbitrary intersections are closed.
4. The closure of the open ball $B_r(a)$ is always the closed ball $B_r[a]$.
False. In a discrete space, $\overline{B_1(a)}=\{a\}$ but $B_1[a]=E$. Only $\overline{B_r(a)}\subset B_r[a]$ holds in general. Equality holds in normed spaces.
5. $\operatorname{diam}B_r(a)=2r$ in every metric space.
False. Only $\le2r$. In a discrete space $\operatorname{diam}B_{1/2}(a)=0$.
6. $f(A\cap B)=f(A)\cap f(B)$ for every function.
False. Only ⊂ holds. Counterexample: a constant function with $A=\{0\}$, $B=\{1\}$. Equality holds when $f$ is injective. Inverse images always preserve ∩, ∪ and complements.
7. Writing $f^{-1}(C)$ requires $f$ to be bijective.
False. The inverse image $\{s:f(s)\in C\}$ is defined for every function.
8. A symmetric, transitive relation is reflexive.
False. The empty relation on a nonempty set, or $\{(1,1)\}$ on $\{1,2\}$. The "proof" assumes each $x$ is related to something.
9. A continuous bijection between metric spaces is a homeomorphism.
False. $\mathrm{id}:(\R,d_{\text{disc}})\to(\R,|\cdot|)$ is a continuous bijection, but its inverse is not continuous.
10. Continuous on $]0,1]$ implies uniformly continuous.
False. $1/x$ is a counterexample. It is true on closed and bounded intervals (Uniform Continuity Theorem).
11. Topologically equivalent metrics have the same Cauchy sequences.
False. $|\tan^{-1}y-\tan^{-1}x|$ vs $|y-x|$ on $\R$: $x_n=n$ is Cauchy for the first only. Lipschitz equivalent metrics do have the same Cauchy sequences.
12. Topologically equivalent metrics have the same bounded sets.
False. $d$ vs $\min\{d,1\}$ on $\R$.
13. Every Cauchy sequence in a metric space converges.
False in general. In $(\,]0,1],|\cdot|)$, the sequence $1/n$ is Cauchy but has no limit in the space. It is true in $\R$ (completeness of $\R$).
14. Every metric on a vector space comes from a norm.
False. The discrete metric and the $\tan^{-1}$ metric are bounded, but a norm-metric on a nonzero space is unbounded.
15. If $D$ and $A$ are dense in $E$, then $D\cap A$ is dense.
False. $\Q$ and $\R\setminus\Q$. It is true if one of them is open and dense.
16. A metric subspace inherits the "shape" of the space, like linear subspaces do.
False. $\{e_1,\dots,e_n\}\subset\R^n$ is the discrete metric times $\sqrt2$.
17. "$[0,1[$ is open" is a meaningful statement on its own.
Not without saying "in which space". It is open (and closed) in itself, and neither in $\R$.
18. A countable union of countable sets is countable.
True, using countable choice to pick the surjections $\psi_n$.
19. $\Pow(\N)$ is countable.
False (Cantor). But $\Pow_{00}(\N)$, the set of finite subsets, is countable.
20. The LUB Principle is a theorem proved from the field axioms.
False. It is an axiom (completeness axiom). The Archimedean property is derived from it. ($\Q$ is an ordered field without LUB.)
21. Lipschitz ⇒ uniformly continuous ⇒ continuous, and both converses fail.
True. $\sqrt x$ on $[0,1]$ is uniformly continuous (UCT) but not Lipschitz. $1/x$ on $]0,1]$ is continuous but not uniformly continuous.
22. If $x_n<y_n$ for all $n$ and both converge, then $\lim x_n<\lim y_n$.
False. Only ≤ survives: $0<1/n$, but both limits are 0.
23. An isometry need not be injective.
False. Isometries are always injective, since $d(x,y)=d(f(x),f(y))=0\Rightarrow x=y$. They need not be surjective.
24. Continuous maps send open sets to open sets.
False. A constant map. It is preimages of open sets that are open.
25. $\operatorname{Int}\Q=\emptyset$, $\overline\Q=\R$ and $\partial\Q=\R$ in $\R$.
True. This uses density of both $\R\setminus\Q$ and $\Q$.

Flashcards: definitions and statements to know word for word

Say the answer out loud before you open the card. If you hesitate, review that card again.

Metric (all axioms, named)
$d:S\times S\to[0,\infty[$ with (MS1) $d(x,y)=0\iff x=y$; (MS2) Symmetry; (MS3) Triangle Inequality $d(x,z)\le d(x,y)+d(y,z)$.
Norm (N1)–(N3)
Definiteness $\|v\|=0\Rightarrow v=0$; Homogeneity $\|\lambda v\|=|\lambda|\|v\|$; Triangle inequality.
Injective / surjective
$f(s_1)=f(s_2)\Rightarrow s_1=s_2$ / $\forall t\ \exists s:\ f(s)=t$ ($\operatorname{Ran}f=T$).
Left / right inverse ⟷ ?
Left inverse ⟺ injective (if $S\ne\emptyset$ or $T=\emptyset$). Right inverse ⟺ surjective (needs AC).
Axiom of Choice (two forms)
A product of nonempty sets is nonempty. Equivalently, every family of nonempty subsets has a choice function.
Equivalence relation; partition
Reflexive, symmetric, transitive. Partition: covers $S$, blocks nonempty, distinct blocks disjoint. The two notions correspond bijectively.
Canonical decomposition
$f=i\circ\tilde f\circ q$: quotient (surjection), then $\tilde f:[s]\mapsto f(s)$ (bijection onto $\operatorname{Ran}f$), then inclusion (injection).
Countable
There is an injection $S\to\N$. For $S\ne\emptyset$, equivalently a surjection $\N\to S$. Equivalently, finite or bijective to $\N$.
Cantor's Theorem
No surjection $S\to\Pow(S)$. Proof via $D=\{s:s\notin\phi(s)\}$. Also no surjection $S\to\F(S;\{0,1\})$.
Schröder–Bernstein
Injections $S\to T$ and $T\to S$ ⇒ a bijection $S\to T$.
LUB Principle
A nonempty subset of $\R$ that is bounded above has a least upper bound. It is an axiom.
Approximation of suprema
$\forall\eps>0\ \exists a\in A:\ \sup A-\eps<a\le\sup A$. Sequence version: some increasing $(a_n)\subset A$ has $a_n\to\sup A$.
Archimedean property (4 forms)
$\Q$ is order dense; $\forall x>0\ \exists n:\ 1/n<x$; $\forall x>0,y\ \exists n:\ nx\ge y$; $\N$ is unbounded.
Bolzano–Weierstrass
Every sequence in a closed bounded interval $J$ has a subsequence converging to a point of $J$.
Completeness of ℝ
Convergent ⟺ Cauchy ⟺ bounded with $\liminf=\limsup$.
Uniform Continuity Theorem
Continuous on a closed bounded interval ⇒ uniformly continuous, bounded, attains max and min.
Lipschitz map; Lip f
$d(f(x),f(y))\le M\,d(x,y)$; $\operatorname{Lip}f=\sup_{x\ne y}\frac{d(f(x),f(y))}{d(x,y)}$. Strict contraction: $\operatorname{Lip}f<1$.
Isometry; isometric spaces
Preserves distances exactly. Spaces are isometric if there is an isometric bijection.
Diameter; bounded set
$\operatorname{diam}A=\sup d(x,y)$; bounded iff it is finite.
Open ball / closed ball / sphere
$d(x,a)<r$ / $\le r$ / $=r$.
Open set
$\forall p\in U\ \exists r>0:\ B_r(p)\subset U$.
Point of closure (3 equivalent forms)
Every ball meets $A$; a sequence in $A$ converges to $p$; $\operatorname{dist}_A(p)=0$.
Closed set (2 forms)
$\overline A=A$. Equivalently, limits of convergent sequences in $A$ lie in $A$. Equivalently, the complement is open.
Interior; closure as extremal sets
$\operatorname{Int}A$ is the largest open subset of $A$. $\overline A$ is the smallest closed superset.
Boundary
$\partial A=\overline A\setminus\operatorname{Int}A=\overline A\cap\overline{A^\complement}$. It is closed.
Dense; separable
$\overline D\supset A$. Separable: has a countable dense subset.
Global continuity
$f$ continuous ⟺ preimage of every open set is open ⟺ preimage of every closed set is closed ⟺ sequentially continuous.
Homeomorphism (H1–H3)
Continuous, bijective, continuous inverse.
Topological vs Lipschitz equivalence
$\mathrm{id}_S$ is a homeomorphism vs bi-Lipschitz ($d_2\le Ld_1$, $d_1\le Kd_2$). The second implies the first, not conversely.
When is α∘d an equivalent metric?
α definite, increasing, subadditive, continuous at 0. Examples: $cs$, $\min\{s,1\}$, $s/(1+s)$, $\tan^{-1}s$, $s^{1/p}$.
Open/closed sets of a subspace
$X$ open (closed) in $\tilde A$ ⟺ $X=A\cap U$ with $U$ open (closed) in $E$.
Open subsets of ℝ
Every open subset of $\R$ is a countable union of open intervals with rational endpoints.
