Proof toolkit & exam language

How examiners want proofs written
  • Name the axiom or result you use, e.g. "by (MS3), the Triangle Inequality", "by the Approximation of suprema", "by the Sandwich Principle", "by the LUB Principle", "by Bolzano–Weierstrass".
  • TFAE proofs: prove a cycle (i)⇒(ii)⇒(iii)⇒(i). Say so at the start.
  • Set equality $A=B$: prove $A\subset B$ and $B\subset A$ (an "element chase": "let $x\in A$ … hence $x\in B$").
  • Contrapositive: to prove $P\Rightarrow Q$, prove $\lnot Q\Rightarrow\lnot P$. Contradiction: assume $P$ and $\lnot Q$ and derive something false.
  • Well-defined: when you define a function on equivalence classes (or by a formula that might be ambiguous), check that the value does not depend on the choice of representative.
  • Quantifier order matters: "$\forall\eps>0\ \exists\delta>0$" (continuity) differs from "$\exists\delta>0\ \forall x$" (uniform continuity: δ independent of the point).
  • ε-room: to show $a\le b$, it suffices to show $a<b+\eps$ for every $\eps>0$. To show $a=0$ for $a\ge0$, show $a<\eps$ for all $\eps>0$.
  • Sup/inf: to show $s=\sup A$, show (1) $s$ is an upper bound and (2) nothing smaller is: $\forall\eps>0\ \exists a\in A$ with $a>s-\eps$.
