Introduction
In this section we introduce the concept of completeness for
a metric space. This is a property, that a metric space may,
or may not, have—namely that each of its Cauchy sequences
actually converges. [Thus R, for example, is complete as
a metric space, but [0, 1[ and Q (with respect to their rel-
ative metrics) are not.] In particular, the following results
are covered. Let E and E ′ be metric spaces, with E ′ being
complete.

    E embeds isometrically into the normed vector space
     ℓ∞(E; R), and every metric space has a “completion”
    Banach’s Contraction Mapping Theorem, on fixed points
     of a strict contraction
    Cantor’s Intersection Theorem, providing another view
     of completeness
    the Baire Category Theorem, giving the density of count-
     able intersections of open dense subsets of a complete
     metric space (non-examinable).
