Q4.2

Let E be a metric space with metric d, let V ⊆ E be open, and let F ⊆ E be finite. Put U = V \ F .
We show that U is open using the open-ball definition.
If U = ∅, then U is open. Otherwise, take any p ∈ U . Since p ∈ V and V is open, there exists
r > 0 such that
                                        BrE (p) ⊆ V.
If F = ∅, then U = V and there is nothing more to prove. Hence suppose F ̸= ∅, and write
F = {a1 , . . . , am }.
Since p ∈
        / F , we have d(p, ai ) > 0 for every i = 1, . . . , m. As there are only finitely many of these
distances, their minimum
                                          δ := min d(p, ai )
                                               1≤i≤m

is strictly positive. Define
                                          t := min{r, δ} > 0.
We claim that BtE (p) ⊆ U .
Let x ∈ BtE (p). Then d(x, p) < t ≤ r, so x ∈ BrE (p) ⊆ V . Also, if x ∈ F , then x = ai for some i,
and symmetry gives
                                    d(x, p) = d(p, ai ) ≥ δ ≥ t,
contradicting d(x, p) < t. Therefore x ∈
                                       / F , and hence x ∈ V \ F = U .
For every p ∈ U , we have found t > 0 such that BtE (p) ⊆ U . Thus U is open.
