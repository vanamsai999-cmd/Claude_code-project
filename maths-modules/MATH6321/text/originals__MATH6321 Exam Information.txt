                        MATH6321 Exam Information



The exam will last 2 hours and will feature 5 multi-part questions (covering the content of Chapter
1, Chapters 2-3, Chapter 4, Chapters 5-6 and Chapter 7 respectively). The reason for this is not
to make things difficult for you, but to allow you to play to your strengths!

None of the proofs in the course are examinable...most parts of each question will be computational
in nature, i.e. show me that you know how this scheme/algorithm works in practice. Always provide
full working out where possible. Any valid method of doing such computations will be awarded
marks, unless the question asks for a specific algorithm to be used.

A calculator is allowed in the exam and a Useful Information sheet will also be provided (which
will also be included on moodle in advance of the revision session). Despite this, I will try and
make sure that the numbers are relatively small.

A mock exam will also be provided in advance of the revision session, so that you can see the
overall style of the questions and have a go yourself before we meet up.

Some questions will have parts that require you to give a description of how certain crypt schemes
work, or to compare and contrast the efficiency/security of certain schemes. Remember to be as
precise as possible.

I will not expect you to remember the full details of every single crypt scheme we covered...some
are non-examinable and some will be outlined on the Useful Information sheet. I’ll make it clear
below which schemes need to be remembered for the exam and which will be provided on the Useful
Information sheet.

As always, there will be a couple of parts of questions that are unseen. These are there to test
your problem solving skills and your ability to use results from the course in unfamiliar territory.
For example, you might be asked to explain weaknesses of certain public key schemes, e.g. why a
certain key choice is completely insecure. There are many exercises on this that we didn’t have
time to do in the tutorials...so it’s worth going back and getting some practice!

Below is a list of core skills covered in each relevant chapter of the notes. Practicing these skills
in advance will give you a much easier time in the exam. (Although as mentioned above, you will
need to be able to apply your knowledge too!)

If you have any questions at all, about either the content of the course or the format
of the exam, then please get in touch!

1     Classical Cryptography: A brief history
    1. Explain the Caesar, Monoalphabetic Substitution, Vigenère and One Time Pad ciphers.

    2. Encrypt/decrypt messages using each type of cipher.

    3. Discuss simple advantages/disadvantages of each type of cipher relative to the others.

    4. Calculate the (Mutual) Index of Coincidence for simple (pairs of) strings.

    5. Use a table of Mutual Index of Coincidence values to predict the most likely Vigenère keyword.



2     Mechanical Cryptography: An industrial revolution in
      security
    1. Briefly understand what the components of the Enigma machine do and how they fit together
       in the machine.

    2. Do simple combinatorial computations to figure out how many machine settings exist (for a
       simplified version of the machine).


The content of the Attacking Enigma section is non-examinable.



3     Linear Feedback Shift Registers
    1. Understand what an LFSR is and how to use one to generate pseudorandom bit strings.

    2. Know how to use the output of an LFSR to encrypt/decrypt either a bit string or a black
       and white picture.

    3. State and use the main theorems to calculate the period of an LFSR for various initial fills.



4     Public Key Cryptography: Asymmetric security in the
      digital era
    1. Explain how Diffie-Hellman Key Exchange works and demonstrate with numerical examples.

    2. Explain how the RSA scheme works and demonstrate with numerical examples.

    3. Explain how RSA digital signatures are performed and demonstrate with numerical examples.

    4. Encrypt/decrypt/sign using the El-Gamal scheme
       (provided on the Useful Information sheet).

    2. Briefly understand how an instance of the Knapsack problem can be interpreted as a Short

    4. Break the Simple Noisy Scheme scheme by using Gauss reduction on a suitable rank 2 lattice.
